# Profile matching (Development only)

The first match engine is a deterministic, explainable comparison, not a prediction of hiring success. It never rejects, sorts or hides applicants. It uses the submitted snapshot for recruiter and candidate application views; the candidate's pre-application job view uses a current eligible profile preview. For old snapshots without matching inputs, the engine can compare the submitted candidate snapshot against the job’s current requirements after rechecking access. Both parties are told these requirements may have changed; historical city identity is unknown. It never substitutes the candidate’s current live profile.

Version 1 considers required skills (45), preferred skills (15), minimum years of experience (25), and work preferences (15). Each listed skill receives an equal share of its group's weight. A self-reported level at or above the job's minimum meets that criterion; a lower listed level does not. An unlisted skill is unknown, not a failure. Minimum experience is compared to total self-reported years, not relevance or seniority. For a remote job, the candidate's remote preference is compared; for onsite/hybrid jobs, matching city IDs meet the criterion and all other cases are unknown (relocation or commuting must be discussed). No demographic, compensation, activity or hidden contact information is used.

A dimension absent from the job does not apply. Coverage is the assessed weight divided by the applicable weight; unknown criteria reduce coverage. The score is matched assessed weight divided by assessed weight. Show the calculated percentage, including 0%, whenever at least one criterion can be assessed. Prominently flag coverage below 70%; a high score with low coverage is not a full-profile match. When no criterion can be assessed, show no percentage. Always show coverage and the criteria; this score is **not** the percentage of all job requirements met and is not a likelihood of success. Weights and the low-coverage warning are provisional, not calibrated or validated.

Requirements and inputs are captured in the consented job snapshot and included in the review fingerprint. The snapshot records algorithm version 1. Jobs with no assessable criterion, missing input, inaccessible snapshots or unsupported future algorithm versions display no percentage. Matching does not make unavailable jobs or private applications accessible. Withdrawal and snapshot purge remove access to application match data; authorized Studio access and phone restrictions are unchanged.

Before production, verify snapshot integrity, concurrent editing, consent/privacy and authorization; validate scoring choices, fairness and coverage across varied candidates; review applicable legal requirements and ensure genuine human review. A profile match is not an automated hiring decision. No production readiness is implied.

Job browse cards show a private current-profile score to an authenticated candidate in Development when the job is eligible for a new application; already-applied or otherwise ineligible jobs show “Match unavailable” there, and the application detail remains the place to see an existing application’s match. The Studio applicant list shows the snapshot-based score, or the clearly labelled current-job fallback for legacy snapshots. Card scores include coverage and do not change list ordering. Both list reads independently recheck their authorization and visibility constraints.

## Proposed next-version foundation (not connected to recommendations)

The existing V1 snapshot algorithm still uses the original 45/15/25/15 weights and the same shared weighted arithmetic. `Features/Matching/RoleMatch` introduces a pure, separate-input comparison contract for future live recommendations, with a validated policy value (`MatchPolicy.Initial`, proposed 40/10/20/15/10/5). It uses canonical skill IDs, distinguishes claimed/supported/verified evidence in the explanation without changing the score, compares minimum years, remote/same-city preferences and expected salary versus the annual LPA maximum, and keeps unknown information out of the score while reporting coverage. It does not inspect current salary, personal identifiers, profile completeness, activity or Premium. Its caller must first normalize pay and authorize all inputs and explanations.

Relevant work is explicitly unassessed, not fabricated; seniority bands, structured project relevance, preferences beyond remote/same-city, candidate and Studio retrieval, plan entitlements, configuration storage, Admin controls and ranking are not implemented. The proposed policy is a code value, not an Admin-configurable or persisted policy, and it is not used by any UI or existing application snapshot. Do not activate it for real data until it is integrated with privacy-filtered inputs, version governance, explanation review, fairness and PostgreSQL/build verification. Premium visibility must remain separate and cannot modify this match score.

## Candidate job recommendations (Development only)

`CandidateJobRecommendationService` selects at most 50 visible, eligible jobs sharing a listed skill at the requested proficiency, then applies the existing V1 live-profile score, not the proposed V2 policy. It requires at least one matching skill, a score of 60 or above and at least 70% criteria coverage; it returns up to six, with explanatory criteria. This is a bounded relevance-first pool, not a complete or globally optimal ranking. The user can still browse all open jobs; missing or low-coverage data is not an automatic rejection. The provisional score and coverage thresholds can be changed in the Development Admin recommendation settings; they default to 60 and 70. They are stored as one audited app setting, cached up to 60 seconds per application instance, and invalid policies disable the recommendations response. The skill requirement and bounded pool remain fixed. This is not a versioned match-weight policy or plan entitlement system. Premium, activity, account age and completeness do not influence this ranking.

The service checks an active Candidate, excludes blocked Studios and prior applications, requires active/unexpired jobs and active verified Studios, reuses the public catalog and scoring authorization, then rechecks scores before returning. Individual application submission still rechecks access and eligibility. A change after a response starts cannot retract it. No Studio-to-Candidate recommendation or Premium integration is included. Development-only; database query translation, load, privacy and fairness verification remain outstanding, and no real personal data should be used.

Recommendation settings are accessible only to active administrators on the landing portal in Development and require an audit reason. They affect recommendations, not match score calculation, quotas, Premium or access checks. Policy edits can take up to one minute to propagate across application instances. The V1 score continues to use its original immutable weights.

## Studio candidate suggestions (Development only)

From an active, unexpired job with application capacity, an active Owner, Recruiter or Viewer of that specific verified, non-suspended Studio can view a small set of live discoverable profiles. The query checks active Candidate accounts, profile deletion, the existing discoverability flag and completeness threshold, excludes candidates marked not looking, blocked Studios and previous applications, and requires a listed skill at the requested proficiency. It rechecks Studio membership and profile privacy in the same database statement that reads the profile data. It returns name, headline and V1 fit criteria only—not contact information, compensation, portfolio content or snapshots. These are not applicants and there is no contact or messaging action. Already-applied candidates, including withdrawals, are deliberately excluded from this discovery list.

The first 50 candidates are preselected by shared skills; up to six meeting the Studio-side score and coverage thresholds are shown. These default to 60% and 70% and can be changed independently in the Development Admin recommendation settings. They are stored as an audited app setting and cached for up to 60 seconds per instance. Invalid settings make that direction unavailable until an Admin saves valid values. This is not exhaustive or a global rank. Premium, activity and completeness beyond the existing eligibility threshold do not alter score or ordering. Changes after a response starts cannot retract it. No production use or real personal data until the query translation, privacy, fairness, concurrency and load gates are verified.

Both directions share the same threshold predicate and require at least one listed skill meeting the requested proficiency, even if the minimum score or coverage is set to zero. Thresholds filter suggestions only; they do not adjust V1 weights, override privacy, bypass Studio verification or create an automated hiring decision. An Admin can repair either invalid policy from the settings page without changing the other.
